## 2. Part A: stored bite written in advance

**Problem.** When an enemy enters the square beside an idle Bane cell, the enemy bites first 47.4% of the time beside a
holder and 64.8% beside a cell resting under 5 energy (`m-r1-first-contact.txt`, table 2). A cell whose stored bite
already points at the square is bitten first 2.0% (`m5-refill-killer.txt`, no other enemy). About 45 such contacts per
game; first-order estimate about 12 extra first bites per game, up to about twice that (inferred).

**Change (rule corrected 2026-11-13).** On the rest paths that have an empty neighbour (prey hold, settled rest
below 5 energy, blocked walker below 5): when gate 1 sees a refill pattern (its `thr_tbl` entry is 12: an empty
neighbour with an enemy beside or beyond it), store a bite on gate 1's first-choice square (its `act_tbl` entry). With
no refill pattern, write nothing, so a killer keeps its bite on the square it emptied and far cells are untouched. An
enemy that enters the stored square is then bitten by the 4-instruction stored-bite check at `start:`. Reach: 74% of
enemy entries beside a holder go into gate 1's first choice (`m-r1` table 2b), but that count includes squares with no
refill pattern, where the rule writes nothing; the true reach is the coverage count in section 2b.

**Implementation (review 2026-11-13).**
1. **A separate rest tail.** Leave `rest:` alone. Only the three exits that have an empty neighbour jump to a new
   tail: settled `jb rest`, threat `jb rest`, and the threat fall-through (hold). Boxed and feed-fallback rests keep
   their counts and cannot write.
2. **Registers on entry to the tail.** Threat paths: `ecx` still holds the gate mask and `al` the `act_tbl` entry
   (`VIEW_X` loads after `jnz threat`): about +5 instructions, no recompute. Settled and blocked walker: `al` is
   valid, `ecx` is not (overwritten with `VIEW_X`): recompute the mask in the tail, about +8. Do not save the mask
   earlier: an extra instruction before the gate exit or on a rule-4 path would lengthen racing paths and move the 39
   walker-move marker.
3. **Write only when `thr_tbl[mask]` = 12** (refill pattern). Otherwise write nothing, so a killer keeps its bite.
4. **Byte layout.** `act_tbl` gives `ACT_SPLIT + dir`; the stored bite needs [2] = `ACT_EAT + dir` and [3] = view
   idx + 8. Use a 4-word table indexed by `act & 3`, the same words as `lock2_tbl` (0x0f30 UP, 0x1531 RIGHT, 0x1932
   DOWN, 0x1333 LEFT), and one word store to [2]. Storing the `act_tbl` byte would make the 4-instruction path return
   a split onto an enemy. Not a per-mask word table: the tables are 18.5 KB now and a per-mask word table adds 15.2 KB.
5. **End with `mov al, ACT_REST`** (the word load overwrites `al`).
6. **Do not test the stored square for "empty".** `CELL_EMPTY` is 0 and so is "no stored bite" (byte [3] = 0 reads
   view byte 24, always 0), and an inherited bite (copied as a relative offset) can point at any empty square.
7. **Unchanged:** the late split (`VIEW_TICK >= 2980`, `jae EXIT_ADDR` stays ahead of the tail) and every path that
   leaves through `EXIT_ADDR`. Only `cmova` / `cmovae` are on the site's allowed list.
8. Wall squares cannot be stored (the first choice is an empty square). A child copies the stored bite: fine, the gate
   child enters the first-choice square, so its inherited bite points along its travel.

**Vertical beyond: added to the tail.** `kRefill` reads view idx 6..21, so "beyond" exists only for LEFT / RIGHT
(idx 10, 14). An enemy straight above or below (idx 2, 22) makes no refill pattern. The coverage count (2b) shows it
pays for resters, so the tail also stores UP when idx 2 holds an enemy and UP is empty, and DOWN for idx 22, after the
`thr_tbl` test fails (gate order kept: a refill-pattern square first). Rest tail only: no racing cost; the gate tables
and the refill decision itself are unchanged. Tests: the table-driven test covers idx 2 / 22 too.

**Known small loss.** A killer with two or more refill squares whose emptied square is not the first choice has its
bite moved: about 8% of entries beside a resting non-holder (`m-r1` 2b). Accepted.

**Cost.** Racing paths unchanged: stored bite 4, first bite 12, refill 16, split 16, walker move 39, late split 31.
Settled / walker rests about +8, threat rests about +5; exact counts from `check_cells`. Open (not checked in the
docs): a rest that runs before a bite might save a cell at energy 2; it matters only against bites costing 32-45.

### 2b. Coverage count (existing replays; run before building)

`first_contact.py` columns `rs1` / `rs2`: for enemy entries beside a cell that rested on the previous tick (hold,
rest under 5, other rest; no other enemy on an edge), did the rule store the entered square? `rs1` as briefed, `rs2`
with idx 2 / 22 added. Result (`m-r1b-coverage.txt`, 81 replays, entries with no other enemy on an edge):

| Our state on the previous tick | n | covered as briefed | with 2 / 22 | enemy first now | first-order flips (% of n) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| hold | 3,469 | 78.6% | 78.7% | 47.4% | 9.4 / 9.4 |
| rest under 5 | 1,600 | 57.2% | 71.8% | 64.8% | 17.3 / 21.8 |
| other rest (e >= 5, no diagonal enemy) | 3,447 | 75.9% | 94.7% | 22.5% | 1.9 / 3.2 |
| hold, Siect-F | 724 | 93.0% | 93.0% | 92.8% | 1.5 / 1.5 |

Among covered holder entries the enemy bites first 49.8% now, the same as all holder entries: coverage is not biased
toward easy cases.

## 3. Part B: opening

- Default: "first child long 8/2, emptiest side" (`tools/search/opening_search.py`, `make(dir_rule='open')`). Solo from
  the 11 sampled start squares (`m-solo-matrix.txt`): never below v2.2 at any square or tick; t60 +5.2% (equal starts),
  +7.7% (bad), +4.5% (good); window-a boxed 11.8 -> 10.1% (equal).
- A short solo search around "open-long 16/2, stop after 4 steps with no friend in view" may replace it only if it
  wins clearly: never below v2.2 from t20 to t60 and at most -1% at t100, on fresh start squares (not the 11 it is
  tuned on). Solo cannot show a longer walk meeting enemies while thin, so 8/2 stays the default unless a variant wins
  clearly.
- The acceptance rule applies to the real cell itself, not to the sketch (sketch tolerance plus the t100 margin could
  stack below v2.2).
- No instruction ahead of `jnz threat`; bytes [2]-[3] untouched by the opening; state the walker-move count (39 or new).

## 5. After an upload (the user decides whether and when)

Judge by measures on the first 25 replays, not by the rating. Targets are first-order estimates from the coverage
count (2b) and the s15 flip rule (5-15 bites flip, = 4 half, < 4 none):

| Measure (script) | v2.2 | Target |
|---|---|---|
| Enemy bites first entering beside a holder (`first_contact.py`) | 47.4% | about 38% (47.4 - 9.4) |
| Same, beside a rester under 5 energy | 64.8% | about 43% (64.8 - 21.8) |
| Share of such entries where the stored square = the entered square (`first_contact.py`) | - | about 79% (holder), 72% (rest under 5) |
| Enemy bites first when our cell is armed on that square (`m5` style) | about 2% | about 2% |
| Holds from 5-7 energy that end lost (`gate_split.py`) | 60% | < 50% |
| Window-a boxed share, equal squares (`budget.py`) | 11.8% | the chosen opening's solo value |
| Siect-F games | 0 of 4 | no change expected |

Update the marker costs in `order.py`, `race.py`, `duel.py` by match id first (first bite 12, rest paths longer).

## 6. Decisions

1. Upload: the user decided 2026-11-13 to build and check locally only; the upload (bundle or split) is decided after.
2. Siect-F refill-delay measure: run 2026-11-13 (`m8-refill-delay.txt`, `m8b-refill-delay-cuts.txt`); a delayed
   refill is not a Bane lever: when we do not refill at once, another colony takes the square on the next tick 72%
   of the time (Siect-F 89.6%), and the effect differs by rival (tuning to one rival is excluded).
3. Sandbox games against ladder cells: no (standing rule: no games against rivals).

## 7. Honest size

Incremental. It reaches the close equal-start games (two of five t100 gaps are -154 and -188). It does not close the
large gaps, the bad starts (our opening near walls: solo reproduces them) or Siect-F (bites under 4 from the first
tick). Confidence that v2.3 flips at least 1 in 5 equal-start games against VarSi-type rivals: 40% (deep review).
